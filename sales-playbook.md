# Blank Studio — sales and launch playbook (working concept)


## 1. Positioning

**Who we help:** small and growing businesses, independent professionals, and founders across Puerto Rico.

**What we do:** combine creative direction, design, and practical technology to build clear brands, useful websites, custom digital tools, and connected workflows.

**The problem:** businesses often have fragmented online information, unclear digital experiences, or repetitive manual processes that slow their work down.

**One-sentence pitch (Spanish):** “Ayudamos a negocios en Puerto Rico a convertir buenas ideas y procesos complicados en marcas claras, experiencias digitales y herramientas útiles.”

**One-sentence pitch (English):** “We help businesses turn good ideas and complicated processes into clear brands, digital experiences, and useful tools.”

Start with websites and small, clearly scoped digital projects. Expand into custom software and automation as delivery skills and customer evidence grow. Do not promise work beyond current capabilities.

## 2. Starter pricing hypotheses

| Offer | Starting price | Included |
|---|---:|---|
| Website Launch | $399 one-time | One page, up to five sections, responsive design, contact path, basic on-page SEO, one revision round |
| Business Website | $699 one-time | Up to five pages, gallery, inquiry form after it is configured and tested, basic on-page SEO, two revision rounds |
| Website Care | $59/month | Up to one hour of small updates monthly, basic link checks, email support; excludes rebuilds and third-party fees |
| Brand identity, custom software, automation | Quote | Discover the need, define deliverables, estimate scope, then send a written proposal |

Founding offer: first two one-page websites at $199 only if the scope is clear and the customer permits display of the work. Ask for an honest testimonial, never a positive review. Return to standard pricing after the first two projects.

Collect 50% upfront and 50% before launch/handover. The client should own and pay directly for their domain, hosting, email, premium integrations, and third-party services wherever practical. Confirm tax treatment with a Puerto Rico CPA.

## 3. Customer acquisition

### Prospecting routine

1. Select one initial niche (for example, A/C technicians, plumbers, electricians, or small professional services) while serving businesses island-wide.
2. Find prospects through public business directories, Google Maps, Instagram, Facebook, referrals, and local business groups.
3. Track business name, town, public contact method, website status, one personalized observation, outreach date, reply, and next step.
4. Contact 10 relevant businesses each weekday with individualized, respectful outreach. Do not send identical bulk spam.
5. Offer a short, no-pressure conversation and show a clearly labelled concept demo.
6. Follow up once if appropriate. Stop if the business declines or does not want further contact.

### First outreach message — Spanish

Hola, [nombre]. Vi el perfil de [negocio] y noté que ofrecen [servicio específico]. Estoy desarrollando Blank Studio, un estudio creativo de tecnología que ayuda a negocios en Puerto Rico a presentar sus servicios con claridad y aprovechar mejor sus herramientas digitales.

Me gustaría enseñarte una idea de cómo podría verse una página web para [negocio], con información clara, diseño adaptable a celulares y una forma sencilla de contactarte. ¿Te interesaría verla? Sin compromiso.

### English version

Hi [name], I came across [business] and noticed you offer [specific service]. I'm developing Blank Studio, a creative technology studio helping Puerto Rico businesses present their services clearly and make better use of digital tools.

I'd love to show you an idea for a website for [business], with clear information, a mobile-friendly design, and an easy way for customers to contact you. Would you be open to seeing it? No pressure either way.

## 4. Discovery questions

- What service or product do you most want customers to ask about?
- Which towns or areas do you serve?
- How do customers find and contact you today?
- What questions do customers repeatedly ask?
- Which tasks take too much time or require copying information between tools?
- What action should someone take after visiting your website?
- What logo, photos, copy, systems, and access can you provide?
- What would a successful project look like to you?

## 5. Sales and delivery workflow

1. Qualify the business, decision-maker, need, and budget.
2. Define scope, deliverables, timeline, client responsibilities, revision limits, exclusions, and change-order process in writing.
3. Obtain written approval and the 50% deposit before work starts.
4. Collect approved content, images, brand assets, access, and permissions.
5. Build the agreed solution; keep changes within the agreed scope.
6. Test on mobile and desktop, check links/forms, spelling, accessibility basics, page titles, and contact flows.
7. Obtain approval, collect the balance, and launch/hand over access.
8. Offer maintenance only when the scope and response times are clear.
9. Request an honest testimonial and written permission before publishing client name, logo, screenshots, or results.

## 6. 30-day launch plan

### Days 1–3: Foundation
- Complete brand-name and domain checks before investing in the identity.
- Customize the website and contact details.
- Configure and test the contact form on the selected host.
- Create one polished concept website for a fictional local service business; label it as a concept.
- Prepare an intake questionnaire, written quote, invoice, and scope checklist.
- Verify Puerto Rico business, tax, municipal, and permit obligations with the relevant agencies and a qualified local professional.

### Days 4–7: Prospect list
- Pick one initial customer niche.
- Build a list of 50 relevant businesses across Puerto Rico.
- Record a specific reason each business might benefit from a clearer site or process.
- Begin personalized outreach.

### Days 8–14: Conversations
- Hold discovery conversations with interested businesses.
- Recommend only the service that matches the need.
- Send written, fixed-scope proposals.
- Try to close one project with a deposit; do not do large amounts of unpaid custom work.

### Days 15–21: Delivery
- Gather content and deposit.
- Deliver the first project within the written scope.
- Test, obtain approval, collect the balance, and launch.
- Ask for an honest testimonial and portfolio permission.

### Days 22–30: Improve
- Publish approved work and refine the template.
- Keep prospecting and aim to close another project.
- Measure hours and direct costs per project.
- Adjust the offer and prices based on actual customer response and project profitability.

## 7. Weekly metrics

Track prospects researched, personalized first contacts, replies, discovery calls, proposals, deposits, completed projects, delivery hours, project costs, gross revenue, cash collected, recurring clients, and cancellations.

The key validation signal is not messages sent; it is a customer approving a defined scope and paying a deposit.
